# Playing Shoutan

## Starting out

The first screen asks whether you are new here or have played before.

New players give a name, say how much they have played (from "I've never played" to "Strong (dan)") and can pick an animal as their picture. They can then sign in with their email to play online, or skip that and play offline. Players who have played before sign in with their email, and their name, animal, level and rating come back from the server.

The level you give decides which helps are switched on to begin with:

| Level                          | Switched on                             |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Never played, know the basics  | Territory, Likely areas, Score estimate |
| Beginner, play now and then    | Territory, Likely areas, Score estimate |
| Intermediate, club player, dan | Show Go ranks                           |

The Coach is not switched on by level: it helps a lot, so it comes on, with Territory, when you tap Start a beginner game.

After that the choices are yours. The app remembers them on the phone and doesn't change them again, even when your level or rating changes. The language can be chosen at the bottom of the first screen and later in Settings.

## The main menu

The main menu has Continue (when a game is unfinished), Play the bot, Play on this device and Play online, with Rules, Feedback and Settings at the bottom. Your name in the top corner opens your profile: name, animal, level, sign-in and your finished games.

Play the bot and Play on this device open a setup with the board size, the handicap and, against the bot, its strength from 1 to 7. The strength starts at the level that suits your rating. Players who have never played, or know only the basics, also see Start a beginner game: 9×9, even, against the gentlest level.

There is one game at a time, and Continue returns to it. The game on the phone is saved after every move and comes back after the app or the phone restarts. Starting a new game, or opening an online one, while a game on the phone is unfinished asks first.

## Playing a move

Tap a point to see the stone, then tap it again to play it. If you'd rather play with one tap, switch off "Tap twice to place a stone" in Settings. While your finger is on the board, a see-through stone and two guide lines follow it, and the stone goes where you lift your finger.

Your own picture on the board opens your profile. The bot's picture shows what it is like, and below that how it chooses its moves: the network, the move choice and its rating. In an online game the other player's picture shows their level, rating and how many rated games they have played.

Under the board are Undo, Redo, Settings and Pass. The ☰ menu at the top has Keep playing, New game, Resign and Main menu. Against the bot, Undo takes back your move and the bot's reply together.

Face to face, the screen is laid out for two people sitting opposite each other: White's card and buttons are at the top, upside down, and Black's at the bottom.

## The Coach and hints

These helps are in Settings under Hints, each on its own switch, from the lightest to the Coach last.

- **Coach** asks the bot's network on your turn: its strongest move as a ghost stone (and a second when it is nearly as good), with the reason in plain words when the move captures, saves stones or leaves the opponent's in atari; outlines round stones about to die unless you answer (solid red), likely lost (dashed red) or weak (dashed violet); and what your last move cost when it cost 3 points or more. It also says when a move takes a ko, and lets you hold a stone to see its liberties. A capture gets no note: the stones leaving the board show it. In online games the Coach is off, and greyed out in the game's Settings: the network's ideas would be the bot helping one player.
- **Territory** marks areas that one side has already fenced in.
- **Likely areas** marks, more faintly, where each side is likely to end up.
- **Score estimate** shows each player's expected score on their card. It comes from a few hundred random games played out from the position, so it is rough while much of the board is open.
- **Liberties** marks every empty point next to each group, and a group's last liberty in red.
- **Groups** joins neighbouring stones of one colour so each group reads as one shape.

Holding your finger on a stone shows how many liberties its group has.

## The end of the game

Passing while a lot of the board is still open asks for a second tap first. When both players pass, the app marks the stones it thinks are dead. Where it isn't sure it shows a question mark, and tapping a group changes its mark. Keep playing goes back to the game to settle a group on the board, which costs nothing under area scoring.

Count adds up the score one step at a time: the dead stones, each side's stones and territory, komi, and finally each side's whole area. You can step through it or tap Watch. "Count the score step by step" in Settings turns this off. The result stays under the board, and if the wrong stones were taken off, you can go back and fix them.

## Level, rating and handicap

Every finished game against the bot or online moves your rating, except a game against the bot in which the Coach was on during one of your turns, even once: the Coach plays as well as the bot's strongest level, so such a game says nothing about your own play. Nor does a game against the bot in which you took a move back: the first Undo says so, and a second tap takes the move back. The setup warns when the Coach is on, the result says the game was not rated, and your list of games marks it. An online game that ended before this phone started keeping your rating moves none either, and its result says so. The rating is on the European (EGF) scale, and the app suggests a handicap from the difference between two players' ratings, one stone per rank. Show Go ranks adds ranks such as "12k" beside the names, and after a rated game, against the bot or online, the result shows your rank with a small mark: a green arrow up, a red arrow down, or an amber dash when the rank stayed the same. Start my rating again, in your profile, sets your level anew.

## Playing online

Play online opens the lobby. You sign in once with your email: the app mails you a code, and there is no password. Then you enter the family code once. Until then your games and rating stay only on the phone. After that you can start a game, ask another player who is online to play, or join a game someone else started.

An online game plays on the same screen as an offline one. Beside the other player's name the game shows whether they are online now or when they were last seen, or that your phone is offline and trying again. It waits in Play online while you play another, and in the game's menu, New game opens Play online. Both players agree on the dead stones and both press Count, and then both phones show the same count and result. Each phone checks every move against the rules and a hash of the whole game. If the phones ever disagree, the game stops and a report is sent, so the problem can be fixed.

Your finished games are kept on the phone, and once you have entered the family code also in your account, so they show up on your other phones too. Other players see one rating for you, worked out by the server from your games.

## Feedback

The speech bubble sends a bug report or an idea. The app also saves its own errors. Reports go out once you are signed in, have entered the family code and are connected, and they include the app version and what was on the board.
